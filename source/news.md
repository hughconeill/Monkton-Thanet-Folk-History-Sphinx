# In the News

What's been written about Monkton in the local and regional press —
kept here as it's found, most recent first.

## Monkton Nature Reserve turns 40 (2025)

The Reserve — the reclaimed chalk quarry on Canterbury Road that
comes up repeatedly in Terry Marsh's
[Monkton Memories](history/monkton-memories/19-chalk-pits.md) — marked
its 40th anniversary in 2025. Chalk was first recorded being dug from
the site in 1799, with the last excavation in 1958; Kent County
Council took it on as a waste tip from 1974 before local
campaigners' "vision" turned it into a nature reserve, opened in 1985
by the Reverend Jonathan Aitken. He returned to give a speech at the
anniversary celebration on 28 June 2025. The 16-acre site is now run
by the charity Thanet Countryside Trust.

Sources: [The Isle of Thanet News, 28 June 2025](https://theisleofthanetnews.com/2025/06/28/celebration-marks-journey-from-chalk-pit-and-rubbish-dump-to-40-years-of-beautiful-monkton-nature-reserve/)
and [1 June 2025](https://theisleofthanetnews.com/2025/06/01/monkton-nature-reserve-to-celebrate-40th-anniversary-with-open-day-event/).

## New homes on Monkton Street (2019–2025, ongoing)

A long-running planning story. In **March 2019** developer Heyhill
Land applied to build **49 homes** on former agricultural land behind
Monkton Street. Villagers organised against it: the public
consultation drew 55 objections, and both Monkton Parish Council and
Kent Highways recommended refusal, chiefly over traffic safety —
Monkton Street is already narrow, with the extra road-narrowing
creating "pinch points" close to Monkton Primary School. Parish Clerk
Sara Archer told the council "this scheme and all its inadequacies in
terms of road safety" should be "firmly refused."

The application was refused in **January 2020**. Heyhill appealed,
and — after adding developer-funded environmental mitigation for the
adjoining Special Protection Area/SSSI — won **outline approval in
2021**. As of **December 2025**, the "reserved matters" application
(covering the estate's appearance, layout, scale and landscaping) was
before Thanet District Council; construction has not yet begun.

A separate, smaller scheme for **9 homes** on another Monkton site
followed a similar path — refused in December 2018, dismissed on
appeal in August 2019, then approved in **October 2019** once revised
mitigation was offered. Councillor Reece Pugh and the Monkton
Residents Association (Beth Johnson) both argued at the time that
approving it would "set a dangerous precedent" for the village's
remaining open land.

Partly in response, Monkton Parish Council has applied to Thanet
District Council to have Monkton formally designated a
[Neighbourhood Plan](https://www.thanet.gov.uk/info-pages/monkton-neighbourhood-plan/)
area — the first step toward the parish writing its own planning
policy for future development, rather than responding to individual
applications as they come in.
