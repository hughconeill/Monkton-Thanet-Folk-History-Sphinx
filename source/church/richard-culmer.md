# Richard Culmer (1597–1662)

Richard Culmer — the unlisted vicar of Minster — is buried in Monkton
churchyard, though the exact location of his grave is unknown.

This infamous Puritan minister, active during the Commonwealth period
under Cromwell, was known locally as "Blue Dick" for wearing a blue
cassock instead of the traditional black.

From 1643 he was appointed 'commissioner' at Canterbury Cathedral,
where he was notoriously zealous in "the utter demolishing... of all
monuments of superstition" — destroying priceless stained glass,
sculptures, paintings, and exterior adornments, both at the Cathedral
and in his other parishes of Goodnestone, Chartham, Harbledown, St
Stephen's Hackington, and finally Minster.

The local congregation eventually threw him out, and he came to live
in Monkton with his wife Katherine and their seven children. He died
there on 20 March 1662, taken in by his neighbour the vicar of
Monkton, Nicholas Thorowgood, of Parsonage House.

Culmer was buried two days later in Monkton churchyard, in a spot that
remains unknown and unmarked — a fitting end, perhaps, for a man who
attracted such widespread anger and condemnation during his life.
