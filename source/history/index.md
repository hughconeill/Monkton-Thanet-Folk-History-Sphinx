## Monkton 1926

**A Folk History of Monkton, Thanet, Kent**

Change is all around us. I hope it's possible to strip back the many recent
changes in our village of Monkton (or Monocstun – the "Monks Land") to
reveal the fertile farming community of 100 years ago. My "Centenary Mile
Walk of 1926" (use the pull-out map on the middle pages) will take you along
that journey pointing out the small tied farm-cottages, the large houses,
and the even larger farmhouses. Amongst them are the barns and sheds to
store the harvest of hops, cherries, strawberries, apples, cucumbers,
cauliflowers, arable crops and grazing land for sheep, cows, horses,
(turkeys and chickens). As well as the big farms, there are the small
holdings that were an important ingredient in feeding the village and
exporting to other markets. If there isn't a dwelling to see, then it was a
barn, store, tree, bush, hedge or fence!

Try as I might to be accurate, I'm no expert, so I apologise, and welcome
your personal knowledge to correct me. I'm always happy to talk about this
project. The centenary walk is only Stage 1. I'm still collecting more
stories from Monkton villagers about the life they lived here, and I hope
it will be a fascinating insight for everyone, and a resource for future
generations.

*Stuart Horsburgh*

Originally published in the Monkton News (September 2026).
